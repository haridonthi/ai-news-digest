AI researchers debate how close we are to recursive self-improvement

Dwarkesh Patel · 2026-09-11

Why it matters: A dense technical debate among John Schulman (Thinking Machines), Baron Milich, and Charlie O'Neill on the actual mechanics blocking recursive self-improvement — distillation economics, RL's low bit-rate per episode, and sample-efficiency gaps versus humans — that goes well beyond generic AGI-timeline speculation.

Key takeaways
  • Schulman: RL episodes convey roughly one bit of signal (right/wrong), but mid-training on synthetic reasoning data does ~80% of the work before RL fine-tunes the policy — this is why RL 'works' despite low information content
  • Panel argues distillation is the main force against model-provider centralization: any RL-learned behavior can be cheaply distilled by competitors via router/proxy services collecting prompt-response traces (cited as a factor behind fast Chinese open-model catch-up)
  • Concrete timeline predictions diverged sharply: Baron/Charlie estimate ~2 years for a 10x AI-researcher productivity uplift via automation, while Schulman estimates 5-10 years; full 'ASI-complete' automation of all cognitive white-collar work was pegged at 3-5 years by most panelists, contingent on solving continual/sample-efficient learning (models are estimated to be up to a millionfold less sample-efficient than humans in weight-update learning)
